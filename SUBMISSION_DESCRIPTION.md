This project analyses the genuine UCI Online Retail dataset, a real-world record of 541,909 transactions generated by a UK-based online retailer between 1 December 2010 and 9 December 2011. The dataset was selected because it is real rather than synthetic, independently documented and citable (DOI: 10.24432/C5BW33), large enough to support statistical inference, and operationally realistic: it contains cancellations, missing customer identifiers, extreme wholesale purchases, non-product service lines and pronounced seasonality. These features allowed the project to demonstrate analytical judgement rather than mechanical data cleaning.

The dataset was downloaded from the official UCI Machine Learning Repository, verified against its published dimensions (541,909 rows × 8 columns) and fingerprinted with SHA-256 before analysis. The raw file was preserved without modification. Preprocessing followed a fixed, auditable sequence: 5,268 exact duplicate rows were removed; 1,454 missing product descriptions were imputed as "UNKNOWN"; 9,251 cancellation lines on 3,836 credit-note invoices were separated from sales; 1,336 non-cancellation return lines were isolated; 2,512 zero- or negative-priced lines were excluded from valid sales; 2,910 non-product lines (postage, bank charges, Amazon fees, discounts) were flagged and excluded from product rankings only; and 135,037 rows with missing CustomerID were retained but excluded from customer-level analysis. The final valid sales dataset contains 524,878 lines across 19,960 invoices, reconciling exactly with the 25,900 total invoices. Eleven derived variables were engineered, including revenue, invoice value, product total quantity and calendar features, with recency measured from a documented reference date of 10 December 2011.

Exploratory analysis covered univariate distributions, bivariate relationships, monthly, daily, weekday and hourly time-series patterns, customer, product, geographic and cancellation analysis. Statistical work included a 95% confidence interval for mean order value, Welch t-tests with Cohen's d effect sizes, Mann–Whitney U robustness checks, a chi-square test of independence with Cramér's V, Spearman and Pearson correlations, and an IQR outlier analysis with a winsorisation sensitivity check. Advanced analysis comprised RFM customer segmentation across nine segments, Pareto analysis of customer and product concentration, and an evidence-based business opportunity assessment. Eighteen 200-dpi visualisations were produced and interpreted.

Key findings: net revenue was £10,642,110.80 on 5,572,420 units; average order value was £533.17; 65.58% of identified customers purchased more than once; the United Kingdom contributed 84.59% of revenue; 1,130 customers (26.1%) generated 80% of revenue, so the 80/20 rule is directionally correct but imprecise; Champions represented 21.7% of customers but 64.47% of revenue; cancellations totalled 3,836 invoices (16.12%) and £893,979.73 of gross value; and no transactions occurred on any Saturday in 13 months. Recommendations cover win-back of high-value lapsed customers, basket-building rather than price increases, named-account protection, cancellation reason codes, targeted EU expansion and seasonal smoothing, each with a KPI, expected benefit and stated risk.

The project used Python 3.13 with pandas, NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn, Jupyter and python-docx. It demonstrates reproducible data cleaning, statistical reasoning, customer segmentation, data visualisation, business interpretation and professional report writing. All deliverables — executed notebook, 18 charts, Word report, cleaned-data summaries and this description — are generated from the same verified pipeline. No synthetic records were used and no result was fabricated.
